# Decision log G1: frames, evidence, population (report 14)

Source report: `14-frames-evidence-mechanics.md`. Patch: `patch-G1.jsonl` (16 changes, 1 add, 0 removes, 0 parent edits). Validated with `apply_patches.py` and `tl.compile_taxonomy`; every backticked reference in the patched rows resolves.

## Label decisions

ACCEPT  frame:root_cause_framing  "root cause" is 4,113 eps / 190 podcasts, mostly naming a method of care ("root cause medicine", Hyman 594 eps, Jockers 294) with the contrast implied; the old "needs that contrast" wording would split coders. Method use now counts explicitly; the single-condition causal-claim exclusion is kept. Examples taken from the samples (183016/6, 176668/1).
MODIFY  frame:fear_alarm  accepted the threshold between neutral trend words ("rising", "doubled") and hyperbolic ones ("skyrocketing", "through the roof", "explosion"); 1,192 segments of the latter near disease words (177088/1, 185830/2, 14613/0). Kept "ticking time bomb" as a lower-ranked example instead of dropping it, because it is still the frame when it occurs.
MODIFY  frame:toxin_purity  accepted the exclusion of neutral physiology ("the liver filters toxins", 192686/0, 190143/467). Narrowed the concrete-toxicant exclusion to "without that framing" so that "lead is poisoning our kids" still takes the frame. Dropped the bare single-word examples "toxins" and "clean" (single trigger words); added real framing phrases (193107/1, 185859/4-5).
MODIFY  frame:medical_freedom  accepted the "invoked as a right against pressure, mandates or withheld information" condition and the procedural-consent boundary to `topic:health_system.ethics_law_privacy` (373156/12, 75383/6). Moved the custody "parental rights" point and the Chiro Hustle tagline (~380 eps) to the codebook as health-content scope rules rather than encoding them in the row. Replaced the invented example "parental rights over vaccines" with the plain "parental rights" and the real "we're not really given informed consent" (192433/3).
MODIFY  frame:anti_expert_populist  accepted adding mockery of expertise (sarcastic "trust the science", 205233/4, 206890/2) and the ad-copy example (38168/1). The true-crime "do your own research" exclusion is a health-content scope rule, so it went to the codebook, not the row; added a boundary to `frame:government_distrust` instead.
ACCEPT  frame:maha_framing  "sickest generation/country" now counts explicitly as one element, removing the contradiction between the example and the two-element threshold (439182/1, 174978/4). Kept the old examples (slogan, "sickest generation") as cue phrases, since the definition now says they are not enough alone, and added the two real two-element quotes.
MODIFY  frame:disclaimer  accepted that mandated ad boilerplate counts (DSHEA statement 1,253 eps / 70 podcasts; DTC "talk to your doctor" ~7.8k eps / 235 podcasts): it fits the definition literally, and `relevance: advertisement` keeps it separable in analysis, while a "never in ads" rule would be harder to apply consistently. Replaced "for entertainment purposes" (mostly sleep-story and comedy boilerplate, Get Sleepy 689 eps) with "for educational purposes only" (176628/3).
ACCEPT  evidence:strength_assertion  ad register dominates (14,670 eps); anonymous-authorship formulas ("doctor formulated", "developed by scientists", "scientifically designed") are 2,819 eps / 169 podcasts and had no named home; "gold standard" removed as an example (mostly finance and media, 328844/511, 37730/0); publication record routed to `credential_appeal`.
ACCEPT  evidence:credential_appeal  publication counts used as authority in guest intros (192472/0) are a named person's credential; pairs with the strength_assertion boundary.
ACCEPT  evidence:clinical_experience  added "offered as grounds", mirroring `personal_anecdote`; "my patients/my clients/in my practice" is 7,844 eps and much of it is advice to patients, not grounds (181560/8). Example "my patients" sharpened to "my patients do better when" so the example itself shows grounds.
MODIFY  evidence:traditional_use  accepted widening to ancestral appeals (6,109 eps / 243 podcasts of ancestors, hunter-gatherer and traditional-people terms; 182128/0, 5239/8) and renamed the label (ID kept). Changed the split from the report's: the report put "hunter-gatherers don't get heart disease" here and Blue Zones under foreign_comparison, which gives coders no surface test. My rule: what ancestors or early humans ate or did → traditional_use; outcomes of named populations living today (Blue Zones, Okinawans, Hadza, present-day hunter-gatherers) → foreign_comparison.
MODIFY  evidence:foreign_comparison  accepted adding living-population outcome arguments (175901/6, 195695/2, 181412/3) and renaming to "Foreign or other-population comparison" (ID kept; Loma Linda is a Blue Zone in the US, so "foreign" alone would mislead). Took the present-day hunter-gatherer example from traditional_use per the split above.
ACCEPT  population:children  tweens, preteens and middle schoolers appear with health words in 194 eps / 90 podcasts; the current mapping did not place them. Added "Gen Alpha" per the report's generation mapping.
MODIFY  population:young_adults  accepted the "young people/youth" rule (1,286 eps / 158 podcasts, mostly with no stated age: 331474/0, 181653/0, 13950/7). Tightened to three sentences: minors implied → adolescents; adults implied → this label; no cue or both → both labels.
ACCEPT  population:midlife  sex-plus-age phrases (632 eps / 144 podcasts) take both labels; menopause with no age word stays `population:women` only; added Gen X.
ACCEPT  population:older_adults  boomers added (generations with health words: 381 eps).
REJECT  population:military_veterans  widening to first responders. Evidence is moderate (373 eps / 108 podcasts with true-crime noise, as the report itself says), and merging an occupational group into a veterans label would blur a cleanly countable group (VA, veteran suicide, burn pits) and make the name and ID misleading. Noted under cross-slice notes for a possible separate label later.
ACCEPT  population:racial_ethnic_groups (ADD)  651 eps / 119 podcasts of race/ethnicity group terms near health words, about half health-relevant (~300 eps, well over the threshold); disparities, group-specific rates and race-specific recommendations are a core research dimension with no current home (179083/1, 1138805/3, 166858/0, 318494/1). Definition follows the population-axis pattern and excludes one person's race in their own story. Examples drawn from the samples.

## Codebook

Ordered by codebook section. Each item: what to change, from which part of report 14, and why I judge it sound. Items marked (sync) are needed so the codebook prose matches the label rows in my patch; the codebook currently restates several frame and evidence thresholds and would otherwise contradict the new rows.

### Section 3 (what counts as health content; ads)

1. **Ad test (c), brand names.** Add after the (c) examples: "A free-from or nutrient word that is only part of a product's name ('Coca-Cola Zero Sugar', 'Pepsi Zero Sugar', 'Diet Coke', 'Michelob Ultra') does not pass (c); the ad must state the attribute as a benefit." Report 14, Summary and Codebook notes. Evidence: soda-name set 12,658 seg / 7,763 eps / 224 podcasts; "Zero Sugar" alone 4,911 segments (32032/0, 203758/0, 92955/6). Sound: read literally, (c) turns every soda spot into health content with a product mention, which would swamp ad counts. Note for the editor: if the same ad then states "95 calories, 2.6 carbs" as a benefit, it passes (c) on that statement, not on the name.
2. **Retailers with health claims.** "A retailer advertised with health or free-from claims ('no hydrogenated fats ... allergen-friendly options') passes the test as a topic detection with `relevance: advertisement`; it gets no product mention unless a specific product is named." Report 14, ad-test edge cases (89588/1). Sound; consistent with the retail-venue exclusion in section 8.
3. **Unbranded disease-awareness spots.** "An unbranded disease-awareness spot ('Talk to your doctor about OSA ... provided by Lilly USA') passes test (b): it takes the condition's topic and `frame:disclaimer`, and no product mention." (31661/0.) Sound. Addition of mine: say explicitly whether it also takes `frame:commercialization`; under the current 5.3 wording ("sponsor reads") it does, and I recommend keeping that, since it is a paid read.
4. **Parody ad copy** ("Side effects may include laughing, cheering, and lots of high fives", 1127002/3) is not health content. Sound; add to the Exclude list.
5. **Recurring show taglines and mission statements.** "A show's recurring value tagline or mission statement ('we believe in supporting medical freedom and family health freedom') is the podcast's tagline and gets no annotation; a recurring disclaimer in an intro still takes `frame:disclaimer`." Report 14, 5.3 boilerplate note. Evidence: Chiro Hustle ~380 eps (186239/0, 186253/0). Sound: per-episode prevalence of `frame:medical_freedom` would otherwise be dominated by one show's intro. Belongs next to the existing "a podcast's tagline" exclusion in section 3, cross-referenced from 5.3.
6. **Non-health uses of frame vocabulary** (scope, not labels): "do your own research" about a crime case (mostly true-crime boilerplate: Serialously, Rotten Mango); "parental rights" in custody law (178493/0); "for entertainment purposes only" in sleep-story and comedy shows (Get Sleepy 689 eps) are not health content. Report 14, frame findings. Sound; these are already excluded by the health-content gate, but a one-line example each in the Exclude list will stop coders attaching a frame to them.
7. **Trans athletes.** Sports-fairness talk about trans athletes is health content only when testosterone, physiology or injury risk is discussed; then add `population:lgbtq` + `population:athletes` beside the topic. Report 14, population table (677 eps / 59 podcasts). Sound.

### Section 4.1 (detections; ads)

8. **Where a read ends, and consecutive spots.** Proposed text (report 14, accepted as written): "A read ends after its last line of ad copy: the final call to action, URL or code repetition, or legal tail ('Terms apply', 'Rules and restrictions apply', a gambling helpline, a side-effect list), even when no return transition follows. Content after that is show content. A break often holds several spots in a row: each spot is its own read and starts at its own opening line. A 'Brought to you by [company]' tag after a spot's copy (common in drug ads) closes that spot; it does not open the next one. A host read that continues into the next unit (repeating the code, adding endorsements) is part of the read until the show content resumes." Evidence: return transitions are about a third as frequent as openings (23,438 eps vs 48,585 + 20,668); 70882/6 (Rogan's Visible ad runs straight into the Wakefield retraction, which then carries a `rebutted` narrative), 21273/0, 42250/7, 41892/2, 199798/1, 202451/0, 185859/4-5. This is the most consequential codebook change in the report: without it, `relevance: advertisement` leaks onto show content and stance-bearing narratives get mis-scored.
9. **Rubric step 1** ("mark where any delimited ad read starts and ends") should point to the rule in item 8.
10. **Ad audience lists** ("If you're 45 or older and at average risk", Cologuard 95572/2) take no population label. Already in 5.5; repeat it in the "Topics inside ads" paragraph because Cologuard, Opill and DTC drug spots run in hundreds of non-health shows. Sound, low cost.

17. ### Section 7 (verification candidates; certainty markers)

23. **Marker list additions, `absolute`.** Accept: "for sure" (as a booster), "no doubt", "without a doubt", "no question", "there's no question", "hands down", "certainly", "undeniably", "make no mistake", "the reality is", "it's a fact", "I know for a fact", "I'm telling you", "I promise you", "I guarantee (you)", "trust me", sentence-final "period" and "end of story", the spelled forms "a hundred percent", "one hundred percent", "100 percent", and "it's well known that", "it's well established that", "the science is clear that". Evidence: near health words, the unlisted booster groups are 1,926 eps and 2,227 eps against 3,510 for the listed "definitely"; spelled "a hundred percent" is more common corpus-wide than "100%" (38,671 vs 31,483 eps). Sound: "the reality is" parallels the listed "the truth is"/"the fact is"; "it's well established that" parallels "it is proven that". Note the existing attribution rule (a) already treats "it's well known that" as adoption, not hearsay, so making it `absolute` is consistent.
24. **Marker list additions, `hedged`.** Accept: "I feel like", "in my opinion", "I'm pretty sure", "I would argue", "my guess is", "I suspect", "I assume", "presumably", "chances are", "odds are", "most likely" (see rule 27), "more than likely", "oftentimes", "more often than not", "nine times out of ten", "for the most part", "to some degree", "in some cases", "in a lot of cases", and "certain people"/"certain women" when limiting who the claim covers (like "some"; 192391/3). Evidence: "I feel like" 2,094 eps and the "in my opinion / my guess is / I suspect / presumably / perhaps" group 2,005 eps, each more than the listed "I believe" (1,604). "My guess is" parallels the listed "I guess"; "in some cases" follows the existing quantifier rule.
25. **Marker list additions, `speculative`.** Accept "perhaps" (parallels "maybe") and "I imagine".
26. **New rule: negated boosters are hedges.** "'I can't say for sure', 'we don't know for sure', 'not a hundred percent' are hedges; list the whole phrase as the marker." (24761/5, 183314/5, 23307/8.) Sound; read by form they would be coded as boosters, the opposite of their function. Fits the existing "function over form" rule and the listed "not necessarily".
27. **New rule: "most likely".** "'Most likely' is a hedge when it qualifies the claim ('you will most likely not experience hunger') and, like 'more likely to', a statistical idiom and no marker when it compares groups ('those people are most likely to suffer')." (192469/6 vs 54317/262.) Sound; extends the existing statistical-idiom exemption.
28. **New rule: backchannel agreement.** "Agreement from another speaker ('Yeah, for sure.', 'A hundred percent.', 'Totally.') is not a marker on the claim it answers; code the claim as its speaker stated it." (192469/4, 182452/5.) Sound; also note that "a hundred percent" as health-state content ("he feels a hundred percent", 11960/3) is not a marker.
29. **Modified rule: discourse-opener "obviously/of course/clearly".** Report wording: these are filler before a hedge or opinion. I would state it more generally as a clause-scope rule: "A marker counts only on the clause that states the proposition. 'Obviously', 'of course' or 'clearly' opening a different clause, or introducing an opinion ('Obviously, this is my opinion'), is not a marker on the claim." Evidence 178789/6 (the claim "you are getting enough" carries "chances are", while "obviously" sits on "you need to have protein"), 205344/2; contrast the genuine booster "uric acid, of course, leads to gout" (190504/102). Rationale for the change: the scope rule handles 178789/6 without relying on the mixed-marker rule, and it covers other boosters in the same position.
30. **Settle "suggests" vs reporting verbs.** "'X suggests / indicates that' (research, data, a study) is `hedged` with the verb as the marker; 'X shows / found / showed' is a reporting verb and no marker. An evidential frame introducing the claim ('there's a lot of evidence that', 'research shows that') is not a certainty marker; only the listed proof phrases ('it is proven that', 'the science is clear that') are `absolute`." Evidence: 3,013 segments / 2,578 eps of the suggests/indicates form; 23237/3. Sound; the current text lists "suggests" as a hedge and also exempts reporting verbs, so coders split.
31. **Hearsay "supposed to".** Extend "is supposed to (of a product's claimed effect)" to "is supposed to / it's supposed to presenting any effect as hearsay" ("apparently it's supposed to cause cancer", 23744/3). Add one example: ad copy that attributes its own claim ("nutrients that they say can work ... to fortify gut health", 42105/8) is hearsay distancing → `speculative` with "they say" as the marker. Sound; supplement reads use this hedge often.
32. **Do not extract mandated drug-ad safety statements.** Add to "Do not extract": "the mandated safety statement in a drug ad (side-effect lists, contraindications, 'can harm an unborn baby'); extract the ad's efficacy claims only." Evidence: DTC spots in ~7.8k eps / 235 podcasts, 3 to 8 boilerplate risk claims each (42260/0, 623373/4). Sound: these are regulator-mandated label text, not podcast claims, and would dominate risk-claim counts. I prefer exclusion to the report's fallback of one claim per spot; if researchers want drug-ad risk content, the topic detection with `relevance: advertisement` already records it.

### Section 8 (products)

33. **`product_type` placements.** Add: exercise and recovery equipment (bikes, rowers, massage guns, compression boots, branded saunas, cold-plunge tubs and red-light panels) → `device_or_wearable`; period and sexual-health consumer products (tampons, pads, condoms, lubricants) → `personal_care_or_cosmetic`, or `medication` when regulated as a drug (Opill); training, meditation and doctor-booking apps (RP Hypertrophy, Calm, Zocdoc) → `app_or_digital_service`; eyewear, contact lenses and hearing aids → `device_or_wearable`; an unbranded disease-awareness campaign is not a product; a retailer named as an ad's sponsor is not a product unless a specific product is named. Evidence: equipment brands 2,133 eps / 174 podcasts (28504/2); 185859/4, 175578/1, 4501/1, 31661/0, 89588/1. Sound. Keep the existing rule that the generic practice ("cold plunges", "red light therapy") is not a product; only the branded equipment is. I did not adopt the report's sub-clause about app-delivered coaching staying `program_or_course` "only when sold as a course": it is unclear as written, and the existing types already separate an app from a programme.

### Not adopted

- Report's "rarely / seldom / almost always" row in the marker table: no proposal attached. "Almost always" is already an approximated universal (hedged); "rarely" usually states a frequency that is part of the claim, so it should stay unlisted.

## Cross-slice notes

- **First responders** (rejected widening of `population:military_veterans`): 373 eps / 108 podcasts (police and firefighter suicide and PTSD, "epidemic of suicide in first responders" 695/1, firefighter cancer). - **`population:women` / `population:men` rows** keep "women over 40" / "men over 40" as examples; this is consistent with the new both-labels rule in `midlife` and needs no change.
- **Other groups referencing frames I changed**: any group adding narrative examples that rely on "root cause" or "toxins" wording should check the narrowed toxin_purity threshold and the method use of root_cause_framing.
