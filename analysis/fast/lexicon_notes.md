# Fast lexicon: design notes and known ambiguities

1. **Label keys mirror the pipeline.** Keys under `topics`, `frames` and `evidence` are exactly `slugify(name)` from `analysis/topic_labeling.py` applied to the two canonical tables in `topics.md` (50 topics incl. `other_health_topic`, 27 frames, 7 evidence labels), so fast counts join directly to LLM labels on `label_id` minus its `topic:`/`cross_cutting:` prefix.
2. **Precision over recall on single words.** The corpus is dominated by true crime, sports, politics and comedy, so ambiguous single words (`shot`, `period`, `apple`, `cycle`, `dose`, `positive`) are excluded; ambiguous concepts appear only as multi-word phrases (`flu shot`, `period cramps`, `blood sugar`).
3. **ASR spelling variants are enumerated, not guessed.** Brand and acronym terms carry spelled-out forms (`ag one`, `a g one`, `glp one`, `m r n a vaccine`, `twenty three and me`, `bpc one fifty seven`) because Whisper-style output spells numbers and letters inconsistently.
4. **`LMNT` is the worst brand case.** It is transcribed as `element`, which is far too common to include; only `lmnt`, `l m n t` and `drink lmnt` are matched, so LMNT sponsorship counts are a floor, not an estimate.
5. **Terms assume no reliable punctuation.** No term depends on apostrophes, hyphens or casing beyond what the scanner strips; both `they don't want you to know` and `they dont want you to know` are listed where the contraction is load-bearing.
6. **Regex is used only where a term needs it.** The scanner treats a term as a regex when it contains any of `( ) | ? [ ] \ + * { }`; everything else is escaped and wrapped in `\b…\b`. Plain-literal terms are therefore safe to add without escaping.
7. **Narratives are within-sentence co-occurrence patterns.** Each uses bounded gaps (`vaccin\w*.{0,80}autism|autism.{0,80}vaccin\w*`) in both directions, so they fire on one sentence rather than one episode; a `.{0,80}` window is roughly one clause pair in ASR text.
8. **Narratives include contested, not only false, claims.** `covid_lab_leak`, `gender_care_evidence_contested`, `fluoride_iq`, `microplastics_panic` and `insurance_denial_narrative` are marked in their `note` as contested or partly substantiated; the section is "narratives worth reading", not "claims known to be false".
9. **A narrative hit is not a claim endorsement.** Debunking episodes match the same patterns, which is why `correction` exists as a separate axis — narrative counts should always be crossed with `correction` before being read as prevalence of belief.
10. **Overlap between sections is deliberate.** `seed oils` appears under `food_nutrition`, `maha_adjacent_online_vocabulary` and the `seed_oils_toxic` narrative; sections are independent scorers, not a partition, so per-section totals sum to more than the number of matching sentences.
11. **Topic boundaries follow `topics.md` routing rules, imperfectly.** Where the taxonomy routes a term one way and usage routes it another (cortisol -> stress not mental health; COVID vaccines -> vaccines not COVID; testosterone -> men's health not PEDs), the term sits with the taxonomy but will over-fire on the neighbouring topic.
12. **Known false-positive sources.** `stack`, `protocol`, `optimize`, `hack`, `levers` (optimization framing) match business and tech talk; `trauma`, `narcissist`, `gaslighting` match relationship and true-crime content; `prep`, `dip`, `gear`, `tren`, `meth` are short and collide with ordinary speech, so the shorter ones are constrained (`nicotine gum`, `harm reduction`) or dropped.
13. **`other_health_topic` is a floor detector.** Its terms (`diagnosed with`, `went to the doctor`, `side effects`) are generic health vocabulary meant to find health talk that no specific topic caught; it should be used as a denominator, never as a topic count.
14. **Commercial detection favours the read-out formula.** Sponsor reads are caught by the mechanics (`promo code`, `dot com slash`, `percent off`, `brought to you by`) rather than by product names, so a sponsor absent from `products` still registers as commercial; the `go to \w+ ?(dot|\.) ?com slash` pattern is the highest-yield single term.
15. **Non-health products are controls.** Twelve entries (ExpressVPN, SimpliSafe, Cozy Earth, MyPillow, Squarespace, Shopify, Quince, Aura Frames, Stamps.com, Masterclass, Strava, Ollie) carry `health_related: false` so ad-load can be normalised against non-health sponsorship on the same shows. `helix_sleep` is marked health-related as a sleep product, which is arguable.
16. **Certainty tiers are speaker-hedging proxies, not claim strength.** `may`, `might`, `could` in the hedged tier are extremely frequent and largely non-health; use them only as a ratio within already-matched health sentences, never as an absolute count.
17. **Validation.** `.venv/bin/python analysis/fast/validate_lexicon.py` checks that every label name exists in `topics.md`, that every term compiles under the scanner's escape rule, that no term repeats within a list, that every narrative points at a real topic key, and that product types come from the fixed vocabulary. It exits non-zero on any failure.
