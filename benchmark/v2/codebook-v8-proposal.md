## 1. Recurring ambiguities (ordered by frequency × impact on agreement)

### 1.1 Content words read as certainty markers ("only", "every/all", superlatives, "a lot of")
- **Raised by:** ~75 notes; a/b/c. It is the single largest source of `expressed_certainty` splits on identical text.
- **Representative notes:**
  - "in 'only between eight to twelve grams of fat', 'only' is on the codebook's absolute-booster list, so the claim is coded `absolute`, but here it works as a minimizer" (b/013 ep96366_w0038). The same phrase was coded `unhedged` by c/002 (same window), a/058 and b/067 (ep90375_w0014), and `absolute` by a/059, a/041, b/034 and c/043. That is roughly a 5–5 split.
  - "The certainty rules force `absolute` on the word 'every' in a plain factual description ('a 75 cent surcharge on every vaccine')" (c/013 ep4558_w0011). All 3 coders flagged this window, plus a/006, a/016, b/054 and c/029.
- **Competing readings:**
  - **Literal list.** Any listed word triggers `absolute` (b013, a059, c068 on "the number one reason for death").
  - **Function.** The word is a marker only if it strengthens the speaker's commitment. Coders who took this reading coded `unhedged` for minimizing "only" (c002, b067), temporal "only" ("only stopped listing five years ago", c044, b057), list or scope "all/every" ("all of these work synergistically", c001 vs b057 same window), and factual rankings and superlatives ("the number one killer", "the most common bacteria", "one of the most studied": a049 and b065 `absolute` vs c063 `unhedged` on the same window; "one of biggest epidemics": b011 and c062 `absolute` vs a025 `unhedged`).
  - "A lot of" and "many" counting non-people ("a lot of really great research", "many, many studies", "a lot of B12", "a lot of digestive disorders") were coded `hedged` by some coders (b037, a012, b059) and `unhedged` by others (a046, a014, c016, c042).
- **Proposed rule (§7, "Marker rules", new bullet placed first):**
  > "A word is a certainty marker only when it changes how firmly the speaker commits to the proposition. It is not a marker when it is part of what is claimed. In particular: 'only/just/merely' that minimizes an amount or time ('only 15 calories', 'after only two minutes') or restricts a set ('the only ones that') is not a marker; a universal that fixes the claim's scope or enumerates a list ('a surcharge on every vaccine', 'all the amino acids', 'all of these ingredients work together') is not a marker; a ranking or superlative that is itself the fact claimed ('the number one killer is heart disease', 'the most common side effect is…', 'the first ever…') is not a marker, and 'one of the X-est' is never a marker; 'a lot of', 'many', 'most' are quantifier hedges only when they limit how many people or cases the proposition holds for ('many people', 'most men'), not when they count studies, products or conditions ('many studies show', 'a lot of digestive disorders'). Universals and superlatives remain `absolute` when they are rhetorical ('always', 'never', 'every single time', 'the best thing you can do')."

### 1.2 Marker phrases missing from the lists, and approximated universals
- **Raised by:** ~60 notes; a/b/c.
- **Representative notes:**
  - "'almost every type of stress' — whether 'almost' is an unmarked approximator (leaving 'every' → `absolute`) or a hedge; coded absolute" (c/020 ep190223_w0004). a/022 coded the same window `hedged`.
  - "'early research is suggesting' / 'the evidence suggests': 'suggest' is not a listed marker, so I treated it as a reporting verb (unhedged)" (c/024 ep195560_w0002). b/016 and a/028 coded the same window `hedged`.
- **Unlisted phrases coders had to rule on:**
  - Boosters: "the truth is", "the truth of the matter is", "it's true", "the reality is", "the unfortunate fact is", "that's a fact", "of course", "full stop", "pretty obvious", "very clear", "well-known/known carcinogen", "absolutely" (intensifier or booster), "automatically", "can't possibly", "cannot", "must", "any", "anyone", "forever", "not a single", "strictly limited", "at least", "no matter how".
  - Hedges: "some", "in/for some people", "sometimes", "typically", "normally", "primarily", "in my mind", "I guess", "I bet", "I'm hoping", "my understanding was", "it seems", "seems to", "theoretically", "potential", "not necessarily", "not really", "apparently", "suggest(s)", "links to", "I say it will be".
- **Same-window splits:**
  - "nearly every": a028 and c038 `absolute`, b051 `hedged`.
  - "the truth is" vs "it's true": b053 `absolute`, c066 `unhedged` for "it's true".
  - "apparently": c035 `hedged`, b065 `speculative`.
  - "cannot exist": c052 and b065 `absolute`, a064 `unhedged`.
  - Plain "no" ("no hidden chemicals"): a052 `unhedged`, c060 `absolute`.
- **Proposed rule (§7 marker lists, extend):**
  > "Absolute also: 'the truth is', 'the fact is', 'it's a fact', 'of course', 'obviously', 'without question', 'full stop', 'can't possibly', 'impossible', 'cannot' (as categorical impossibility), 'must' (as necessity of a fact), 'any/anyone/no one/nobody' as universals. Hedged also: 'some', 'for/in some people', 'sometimes', 'typically', 'normally', 'primarily', 'mainly', 'in my view/mind', 'I'd say', 'I guess', 'I bet', 'it seems', 'seems to', 'suggests/suggesting', 'apparently', 'theoretically', 'not necessarily', 'probably not'. Speculative also: 'I'm hoping', 'potentially', 'is supposed to' (of a product's claimed effect), 'I don't know if'. Approximated universals ('almost/nearly/virtually/pretty much every/all') are `hedged`, with the whole phrase as the marker. Plain negation ('there is no X', 'doesn't cause') is not a marker; 'no' is `absolute` only as 'no risk / no doubt / none at all'. Intensifiers stay non-markers even when they look like boosters: 'absolutely' before an adjective ('absolutely critical'), 'really', 'truly', 'game-changer'."

### 1.3 Attribution (hearsay) rule vs "code how the original proposition is rendered"
- **Raised by:** ~35 notes; a/b/c.
- **Representative notes:**
  - "in 'someone who's trying to tell me they already have a cure for cancer', the hearsay-attribution marker rule (speculative) conflicts with the rule for rebutted claims (code the original rendering). I took the original rendering and coded the claim `unhedged`" (b/009 ep71184_w0031). On the same window a/036 and c/002 chose `speculative`.
  - "'as you've heard, seventy to eighty percent of your immune system's in your gut' … works as the speaker's own endorsement" (c/005 ep174944_w0003). Of 3 coders on the same window, a was `speculative` and b and c were `unhedged`.
- **Competing readings.** Attribution words ("someone said", "is considered", "everybody wants to say", "From what I have read", "It's common to hear", "people keep saying", "scientists believe", "it was reported") have been read three ways:
  - always `speculative`;
  - `speculative` only when the speaker distances themselves;
  - never a marker for a rebutted claim.
- **Same-window splits:**
  - "is considered optimal": a031 `speculative`, b020/b045/c027 `unhedged`.
  - "From what I have read": b064 and c057 `speculative`, a057 `hedged`.
- **Proposed rule (§7, replace the "Attribution" bullet):**
  > "Attribution: an attribution phrase is a `speculative` marker only when it presents the proposition as hearsay the speaker is not vouching for ('some people say', 'I've heard', 'supposedly', 'they claim', 'apparently'). It is not a marker when (a) the speaker adopts the claim in the same breath ('as you've heard, …', 'people said X and they were right', 'it's well known that'); (b) the source is a named or identifiable category of authority ('the doctors tell you', 'pharmacists told me', 'the CDC says', 'scientists believe', 'is considered' in a guideline sense); or (c) the claim is being rebutted: for a rebutted claim, code only markers that belong to the original proposition as rendered, never the speaker's distancing ('people say X, which is nonsense' → code X as stated). 'From what I've read/understand' is `hedged`."

Coders a, b and c raised this on 2 windows, about 8 notes.
- **Narratives affected (premise present, conclusion absent, or one listed harm missing):**

  - `sickest_generation` (no "sickest"/"majority"; about 9 notes), `cancer_epidemic_young` (no "ignored"), `covid_vaccine_deaths` ("some died" vs "many"; 3 coders, ep4558_w0011), `long_covid_not_real` ("overdiagnosed"; 3 coders), `pandemic_censorship` ("smeared", no coordination), `lockdowns_worse_than_virus` (costs only; 2–1 split on ep13214_w0015 and ep_synthetic_vaccine_covid).
  - `natural_remedy_cures_cancer` ("fight" vs "cure"; 3 coders), `fasting_cures` ("improves" vs "cures"; 3 coders), `detox_needed` (toxins accumulate, no cleanse named; about 6 notes), `too_many_too_soon` (spacing with no harm language; 3 coders), `birth_control_harms` (libido/UTI not among the listed harms; 3 coders), `glp1_dangers` ("for life" said neutrally, nutrient deficiency; split).
- **Proposed rule (§5.2, new bullet after "The proposition must be present"):**
  > "Core proposition. Each narrative definition states one core proposition (in bold in the table); the rest of the definition and the examples are typical elaborations. Apply the narrative when the core proposition is invoked; an elaboration alone does not invoke it, and the narrative does not require every elaboration. A premise without the contested conclusion is not the narrative (the liability shield exists → not `vaccine_makers_no_liability`; soil is depleted → not `soil_depletion_supplements` unless 'so you need supplements' is said or plainly implied). Verbs of benefit are interchangeable: 'cures', 'treats', 'fights', 'reverses', 'heals' and 'improves [a disease]' all invoke a cure-type narrative; magnitude words in a definition ('many', 'collapse', 'majority') are not thresholds."
- **Table task.** Rewrite each bundled definition as **core** plus elaboration. Examples:
  - `vaccine_makers_no_liability`: core = "Vaccines are unsafe or untested *because* makers cannot be sued".
  - `antidepressants_dont_work`: split, see §2.3.
  - `mthfr_explains_illness`: core = "MTHFR variants cause illness and/or require methylated supplements".
  - `peptides_safe_miracle`: core = "research peptides are safe and broadly healing", with "safe to take indefinitely" accepted.

### 1.5 Implicature threshold: ads, jokes, juxtaposition and missing referents
- **Raised by:** ~45 notes; a/b/c.
- **Representative notes:**
  - "'No seed oils' as a selling point gestures at `narrative:seed_oils_toxic`, but the proposition is not stated, so I did not apply it" (a/059 ep96366_w0038), while "'Ditch the seed oils' inside an ad … coded at 0.6" (b/028 ep178189_w0009). There are about 15 seed-oil ad notes across 6 windows, with 2–1 splits on ep90375_w0014 and ep4558_w0003.
  - "aggressive cancers … 'since 2021', with the vaccine never named. Labeled `narrative:turbo_cancer` at 0.5" (b/045 ep178194_w0005). a/013 and c/068 left it out on the same window.
- **Other recurring cases:**
  - "China, which unleashed a virus from Wuhan" → lab leak (b001 and c044 yes, a063 no).
  - "people dropping dead, cancer is up" after "pushing the vaccine" (5 notes, all at 0.5).
  - The bare list followed by "nobody realized that it was bullshit" (ep9147_w0013): hcq b010 and c041 yes, a030 no.
  - Speech/hearing regression after shots without the word autism (c004 none; a033, b016, a052, b059 `unlisted`).
  - Sarcastic "kids are trans because of the frogs" (3 coders, low confidence).
  - "Balance our EMF exposure" (4 notes, 3 coders split).
  - Bare subject words "structured water", "grounding" (3 coders, 0.5).
- **Proposed rule (§5.2, extend the implicature bullet):**
  > "An implicature counts only when a reasonable listener would take the speaker to be committed to the proposition from the window alone. It does not count when (a) only a product attribute or selling point is stated ('no seed oils', 'PFAS-free', 'non-toxic'); code the frame instead (`toxin_purity`/`naturalness_appeal`); (b) the cause in a causal narrative is supplied only by timing or juxtaposition ('cancers since 2021' with no vaccine named), or by a garbled or ambiguous referent; (c) the subject is merely named ('structured water', 'grounding'). It does count when the speaker uses the narrative's own coined term, because the term carries the proposition ('turbo cancer', 'died suddenly', 'plandemic', 'jab injured'), and when a disparaging imperative presupposes harm ('ditch the seed oils, they're poison'). Sarcasm and jokes invoke a narrative when the joke's point depends on the proposition; code the stance the speaker takes toward it (usually `rebutted`)."
- **Why this rule:** it resolves the turbo-cancer conflict in §4.5.

### 1.6 Discourse role in debunks, mockery, played clips and relayed rebuttals
- **Raised by:** ~45 notes; a/b/c.
- **Representative notes:**
  - On ep178272_w0003, where the hosts mock an RFK Jr. clip, three different topic roles were chosen: reported (a/037), asserted with a correction frame (b/005) and rebutted (c/017). c/017 wrote: "The codebook's rule for debunking passages and its `rebutted` topic rule conflict here."
  - On ep205121_w0027, a played Bill Maher clip that the hosts later endorse, three coder-runs chose `reported_or_quoted` (a008, a040, b046) and three chose `asserted_or_endorsed` (c012, c039, b063).
- **Other splits:**
  - A host reading an FDA rebuttal he rejects (ep14596_w0004; 3 coders, 3 treatments).
  - A relayed third-party rebuttal (the Kenvue CEO; narrative `rebutted`, claim `reported`).
  - Relayed and rejected "bodily autonomy" framing (a029 and b052 `rebutted` vs c045 `reported`).
  - A retracted joke invoking a narrative (b007 `unclear` vs a025 and c060 `rebutted`).
  - A cult's claims relayed sarcastically (topics reported, narratives rebutted).
  - One proposition asserted by one speaker and rebutted by another: one claim or two? (a022, b002, c056.)
- **Proposed rules (§6, add after "A debunking passage …"):**
  > "Stance is the stance of the speaker or show, not of the quoted source. (1) Mockery or ridicule that rejects a proposition is a rebuttal: the narrative and claim are `rebutted`; the topic detection is `asserted_or_endorsed` with `frame:correction_debunking` only if a correction is stated or plainly implied ('that's nonsense, it doesn't work'), otherwise no correction frame. (2) A played clip, read email or quoted source takes the stance the hosts show toward it within the window: `asserted_or_endorsed` if they endorse it, `rebutted` if they reject it, `reported_or_quoted` only if they do neither. (3) A relayed rebuttal (an official denying a narrative) is coded from the speaker's stance toward the narrative: if the speaker relays the denial approvingly, the narrative is `rebutted`; if neutrally, `reported_or_quoted`. (4) A relayed frame the speaker rejects takes `rebutted`. (5) A joke that is retracted takes no claim; any narrative it invokes takes `unclear`. (6) When one speaker asserts and another rebuts the same proposition, extract one claim with the stance of the passage as a whole (usually `rebutted`) and split the narrative detection by span."

### 1.7 `specific_study` vs `vague_research`: what makes a study identifiable
- **Raised by:** ~50 notes; a/b/c.
- **Representative notes:**
  - "'A study in 2012' with a finding is borderline between `evidence:specific_study` and `evidence:vague_research`; I chose `specific_study`" (b/002 ep181065_w0001). On the same window c/035 also chose specific; a/034 chose `vague_research` "because it is not identifiable".
  - The cell-danger-response framework cited by author name (ep190223_w0004): all 3 coders "could not separate" the two labels and coded both.
- **Other recurring cases:**
  - Journal plus finding ("A Lancet paper found"; 4 notes, 3 coders).
  - Design plus size plus finding with no source ("one study of more than a thousand teens", "70,000-women sleep study", "a rat study with three groups"; split).
  - A named scientist plus a finding (Warburg, James Lind).
  - The guest's own unpublished or ongoing trials (ep192615_w0006; 3 coders); ongoing UW/UC Davis trials with no finding (3 coders).
  - A multi-lab replication; Mendelian randomization "studies".
  - "18 studies found", "a recent paper which reviewed a bunch of studies".
- **The codebook's "or" list makes any single cue sufficient.** The table reads: "the speaker gives enough to find it (author, journal, institution with a specific finding, year, design or named dataset)".
- **Proposed rule (§5.4, replace the first bullet):**
  > "`evidence:specific_study` needs a finding plus at least one of: a named author or research group, a named journal, a named institution, a trial or dataset name, or a year together with a design. A design, sample size or year alone with a finding ('a study of 1,000 teens found…', 'a 2012 study noted…') is `evidence:vague_research` (add `evidence:weak_human_evidence` when the design is observational). A named scientist's theory or body of work without a specific study ('Naviaux's cell danger response', 'Warburg showed') is `evidence:vague_research` (plus `evidence:prestige_institution` if a prize or institution is invoked). Trials announced or under way with no finding are not evidence signals. The speaker's own unpublished experiment with a stated design and result is `specific_study` + the kind of evidence (`weak_human_evidence`/`preclinical_extrapolation`)."

### 1.8 Ad delimitation and self-promotion outside a sponsor read
- **Raised by:** ~55 notes; a/b/c.
- **Representative notes:**
  - "the in-show plug for partners Juve and MP Hormones has no clear ad-read delimiter. I still coded it `relevance: advertisement`" (a/003 ep195720_w0006). b006 and c050 did the same, with doubts.
  - "'Maps 15' is probably the hosts' own programme, but the window does not say so. I coded it `recommended`" (b/011 ep195507_w0016). c016 and a046 coded `own_product` with commercialization, using outside knowledge.
- **Recurring cases:**
  - The host's own product pitched in the episode body (10X Focus, Ovi, ENERGYbits, plastic-surgery clinic; usually substantive + commercialization, but Ovi split `recommended` (b003, a023, c046) vs `own_product` (c030)).
  - A scripted lead-in with no opening delimiter (food-dye passage ep185864; at-home blood test ep174944; parasite copy). A one-sentence sponsor tag inside an answer (Gundry, 4 notes).
  - Intro self-promotion (health-coaching pitch; 3 coders `passing`).
  - A health coach promoting coaching in general (a060 and b010 commercialization at 0.5, c057 none).
  - A host plugging a guest's book (about 8 notes; `own_product` agreed, commercialization split).
  - An affiliate code read by the host ("advertised" vs "own").
  - Unnamed own offerings (commercialization but no product).
  - "My top supplements" (5 notes; all `recommended`, uneasily).
  - The podcast's own paid membership, a guest's podcast, a cross-promo for another show, and the host's free content website (drjockers.com, 6 notes, 3 coders).
- **Proposed rule (§4.1 `advertisement`, add):**
  > "A read is delimited if it has any of: an opening or closing transition ('quick break', 'this episode is brought to you by', 'back to the show', 'here comes the show'), a promo code or URL call to action, or a scripted change of register into ad copy. Score the whole delimited read, including a lead-in that the read's copy continues, as `advertisement`. Self-promotion with none of these is `substantive`/`passing` + `frame:commercialization`. `frame:commercialization` applies when any speaker promotes an offering of a speaker present in the conversation (host or guest), including a host plugging a guest's book or clinic; it does not apply to praise of an absent third party's product unless payment or affiliation is stated. Promotion of a profession in general ('a health coach can help you') is not commercialization; an invitation to book the speaker's own service is. Do not use outside knowledge to decide ownership: if the window does not show the product is the speaker's, it is `recommended`."
- **§8 product rule (add):**
  > "A podcast's own episodes are excluded, but a paid membership, newsletter or course sold by the show, another podcast promoted in a cross-promo, and a guest's own show or channel are `book_or_media` products. A free website of articles is a product only if it is promoted as a lead magnet (a named free guide, quiz, test or course)."

A list that appears inside a single sponsor read ('cholesterol to testosterone to micronutrient levels') takes only the advertised product's subtopic."

- **Proposed rule (§3 Exclude, revise the ad bullets):**
  > "A product ad is health content if it (a) sells a health product or service, or (b) states a health effect or risk ('better sleep', 'no stuffy noses', 'without forever chemicals that harm hormones'), or (c) makes a nutrient, ingredient or 'free-from' claim framed as healthier ('zero sugar', 'no seed oils', 'gluten-free', 'only 15 calories', 'high protein'). Pure composition with no health frame ('cage free', 'never from concentrate'), safety features of non-health products (cars, child locks), 'enjoy responsibly' and problem-gambling or helpline boilerplate are excluded. A health condition mentioned inside a non-health ad as a fact about the host ('my social anxiety') is a `passing` topic detection with `relevance: advertisement`; no product is recorded."

## 4. Places coders departed from the codebook or found it contradictory

1. **§7 marker lists vs their purpose.** The text reads: "Universal and superlative boosters are `absolute`: 'always', 'never', 'every', 'all', 'none', 'nothing', 'no risk', 'only', 'the best', 'the strongest', 'the most effective', 'number one'…". Coders split between applying this literally (b013, a059, c068: "I followed the letter of the rule") and departing from it (c002, b067, b057, c044: "I coded `unhedged`"). Also, §7 says "Statistical idioms ('twice as likely', 'more likely to') are not hedges", but b060 coded "the more likely cause" as a hedge, noting "the marker rules conflict here". Fix: §1.1–1.2.
2. **§7 attribution rule vs the quoted/rebutted rule.** The text reads: "a claim attributed to unnamed others or hearsay ('some people say' …) is `speculative`" vs "For a quoted, questioned or rebutted claim, code how the original proposition is rendered, not the speaker's attitude to it". Coders called these directly conflicting (b009, c002, a036, b047, a067, c053). Fix: §1.3.
3. **§6 debunking rule vs the topic-role rule.** "A debunking passage is an `asserted_or_endorsed` topic detection with `frame:correction_debunking`" vs "Topic detections take … `rebutted` when the stretch is the speaker arguing against something". c017: "The codebook's rule for debunking passages and its `rebutted` topic rule conflict here"; a037 and b005 also flagged it. Fix: §1.6.
4. **§5.2 "code the side whose proposition is invoked" vs §5.3 "the corrected claim … if it is a listed narrative, labeled with that narrative as `rebutted`"** for two-sided narratives (HRT). a003: "The codebook's 'code the side invoked' rule and its 'debunked narrative is rebutted' rule pull in different directions." Fix: §3 #41.
5. **§2 "Use general knowledge … (that 'turbo cancer' is a vaccine trope …)" vs §5.2 "do not supply the proposition from what you know about the speaker or the news story".** Coders split on adding `topic:vaccines.covid_vaccines` for "turbo cancer" with no vaccine named (a056 and c070 added it from the gloss; b028 coded the narrative only), and on "cancers since 2021". Fix: §1.5 (coined terms carry the proposition; timing alone does not).
6. **§3 "a victim's gunshot wound and death in true crime" (include, passing)** gives no subtopic and no threshold for bare "murder". There are about 40 notes and the late bundles are dominated by it. Fix: §1.11.
7. **§3 "an analogy to a health subject … : a `passing` detection"** vs §4.1 `substantive` ("discussed, explained or argued … two or more sentences"). A vaccine-mandate analogy argued over many units was coded substantive by a014; a 12-unit insulin analogy was coded passing by a065. ## 5. Highest-value changes (summary)

1. **Certainty markers, function over form (§7).** Words that are the claim's content are not markers: minimizing, temporal or restrictive "only"; scope or list "every/all"; factual rankings and superlatives; "a lot of/many" counting things. This affects about 75 notes, with split codings on identical text.
2. **Extend the marker lists (§7).** Add the ~40 recurring unlisted boosters and hedges. Fix approximated universals ("almost/nearly/virtually every") as `hedged`, and plain negation as no marker.
3. **Rewrite the attribution rule (§7).** Hearsay is a marker only when the speaker does not vouch for it. Named or authority categories are not markers. Rebutted claims are coded as rendered. This resolves 3-way splits on identical windows.
4. **Core-proposition rule for narratives (§5.2), plus rewritten bundled definitions.** Affected: `soil_depletion`, `vaccine_makers_no_liability`, `mthfr`, `peptides_safe`, `antidepressants` (split), `testosterone_collapse`, `sickest_generation`, and the cure-type narratives' "treats/fights/improves" verbs.
5. **Discourse-role rules for mockery, played clips, relayed rebuttals, relayed rejected frames and retracted jokes (§6).** Two windows produced 3-way topic-role splits and a 3–3 split on clip stance.
6. **Implicature rule (§5.2).** Product attributes ("no seed oils") and timing alone do not invoke narratives; coined terms ("turbo cancer") do. This also resolves the §2 vs §5.2 contradiction.
7. **Tighter `specific_study` test (§5.4).** Require a finding plus a named author, journal, institution or dataset, or a year with a design; a design or size alone is `vague_research`.
8. **Ad delimitation and commercialization (§4.1, §5.3, §8).** Define what counts as a delimited read. Commercialization covers any present speaker's offering, including a host plugging a guest's book. No outside-knowledge ownership. Paid memberships and guest podcasts are products.
9. **Health-content boundary for ads and true crime (§3).** Settle food "free-from" claims, vague sleep promises, helplines and conditions mentioned in non-health ads. Bare "murder" is excluded; injury goes to `trauma_fractures`, death to `death_dying`, and `violence_abuse` only for violence as a pattern or issue.
10. **New topic subtopics for the largest gaps:**
    - `metabolic.mitochondria_energy` (~18 notes)
    - `procedures.lab_testing` (blood work, ~12)
    - `food.general_nutrition` (~14)
    - `stress.mind_body`
    - `mental.causes_models`
    - `fertility.conception_general`
    - `procedures.blood_cell_therapies`
    - `cancer_alt.integrative_adjuncts`
    - `vaccines.efficacy_response`
    - `dementia_ageing.cognitive_ageing`
    - `neuro.other_neurological`
    - `wellness.energy_fatigue`

    Also extend `wellbeing_grief_loneliness` to cover mood, and `recovery.red_light` to all light therapy.
11. **New narratives** for the propositions all 3 coders independently coded as `unlisted_narrative`: `alcohol_moderate_protective`, `stealth_infections_root`, `vitamin_a_toxicity`, `heart_not_pump`, `autism_recoverable`, `alzheimers_reversible`, `abortion_infanticide`, `vaccines_for_profit`, `cancer_metabolic_disease`, and an `outbreak_engineered` generalization. Also broaden `vaccines_cause_autism` to developmental regression and `root_canals_amalgams_illness` to implants and cavitations.
12. **Nutrient-status rule.** Levels, deficiency and testing with no form stated go to the nutrient's `supplements.*` subtopic. This ends about 15 notes of food-vs-supplement inconsistency, including the same coder within one window.
13. **Single-keyword frame triggers (§5.3).** Set explicit thresholds for `root_cause_framing` (needs a contrast), `fear_alarm` (stock "epidemic/crisis" excluded), `maha_framing` (two of four elements), `optimization` and `toxin_purity`. Add `frame:industry_distrust` for insurers, health systems, cosmetics and philanthropy.
14. **Population fixes (§5.5).** One person's own condition takes no population label. Add age mappings and `young_adults`, `midlife` and `military_veterans`. Remove military from `athletes` and give `older_adults` an age.
15. **Remove double listings and settle substance-home conflicts in the tables.** L-theanine, forskolin, protein bars, Botox, ivermectin, Function Health, "blood work", "GRAS loophole", "insulin as a hormone signal", "allergy medication". Add a `product_type` guidance line for recurrent products (Eight Sleep → household, telehealth services → clinic, protein bars → food).
